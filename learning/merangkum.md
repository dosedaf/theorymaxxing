# Merangkum format

Summaries alone don't differentiate — a thousand people summarize DDIA.
Every post has four sections:

## 1. The spine

The summary, your words, from your dumps. This is the retrieval work made
public. If the dumps are weak, the post isn't ready.

## 2. One diagram, hand-made

Compress the whole chapter into a single diagram you drew, nothing copied.
This is the pre-write test: **if you can't draw it, you didn't get it.**
Drawing it saves you from publishing a shallow post.

## 3. My take

What surprised you, what the author glossed over, where it applies to your
build. This is the section nobody else's summary has. It's what a hiring
manager remembers.

## 4. The experiment (when possible)

Break something locally, show it. Ideas per post:

| Post | Experiment |
|---|---|
| DDIA: Replication | Kill the primary, show the failover wobble |
| The Log | Start a consumer group, kill a member, show rebalance + offset behavior live |
| ClickHouse / OLAP | Same query against row vs columnar, show scan counts |

Each experiment doubles as thesis reconnaissance.

## Writing rules

- Draft in **one light-session**, from your dumps. Never in a deep block.
- Never same-day polish.
- Publish at 90%. Perfectionism is input hoarding wearing a nicer outfit.
- Cadence: 1 post every 1–2 weeks. Consistency beats length.
